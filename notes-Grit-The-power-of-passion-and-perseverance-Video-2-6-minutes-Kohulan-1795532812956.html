GRIT: THE POWER OF PASSION AND PERSEVERANCE Angela Lee Duckworth – TED Talk I. Introduction to Grit A. Teaching experience i. At the age of 27, Angela Duckworth left management consulting to become a teacher. ii. She taught seventh-grade mathematics in public schools in New York City. iii. She observed differences in students' academic performance. a. Some excellent students had average IQ scores. b. Some highly intelligent students performed poorly. B. Understanding learning i. Difficult subjects could be learned through sufficient effort and time. ii. Learning depends on motivation and psychological factors. a. Intelligence alone cannot determine academic success. 1. Students can learn if they work hard and long enough. II. Research on Success A. Psychological studies i. Duckworth pursued psychology to understand the reasons behind success. ii. She studied people in challenging environments. a. West Point Military Academy – predicted cadets who would complete training. b. National Spelling Bee – studied children's success in competitions. c. Tough neighbourhood schools – studied teacher performance and retention. d. Sales companies – studied employees' job retention and earnings. B. Findings of the research i. Grit emerged as a significant predictor of success. ii. Success was not mainly determined by: a. Social intelligence b. Physical appearance and health c. IQ 1. Grit was the common characteristic linked to success across different fields. III. Meaning and Importance of Grit A. Definition of grit i. Grit is passion and perseverance towards long-term goals. ii. It involves stamina and sustained effort. iii. It means working consistently towards a future goal. a. Maintaining commitment for years. b. Continuing despite difficulties and setbacks. B. Characteristics of grit i. Grit requires consistency and dedication. ii. It involves long-term commitment rather than temporary enthusiasm. a. Life should be approached like a marathon, not a sprint. 1. Continuous effort is more important than short-term excitement. IV. Grit in Education A. Study in Chicago public schools i. Thousands of high school students completed grit questionnaires. ii. Their graduation rates were observed after more than a year. B. Research results i. Students with higher grit were more likely to graduate. ii. Results remained significant even after considering: a. Family income b. Standardised test scores c. Students' sense of safety at school 1. Grit is important for academic achievement, especially among students at risk of dropping out. V. Developing Grit A. Challenges in developing grit i. Scientists have limited knowledge about how grit can be developed. ii. Parents and teachers seek ways to build work ethic and motivation. a. Maintaining motivation for long-term goals is difficult. B. Talent and perseverance i. Talent alone does not guarantee success. ii. Many talented individuals fail to fulfil their commitments. a. Grit is often unrelated or inversely related to talent. 1. Hard work and perseverance are essential beyond natural ability. C. Growth mindset i. Developed by psychologist Carol Dweck. ii. Belief that learning ability is not fixed and can improve through effort. a. The brain develops in response to challenges. b. Failure is not a permanent condition. 1. Students with a growth mindset are more likely to persevere after failure. VI. Conclusion A. Building grit i. Growth mindset is a promising way to develop grit. ii. Further research is needed to identify effective methods. a. Test ideas and measure their effectiveness. b. Accept mistakes, learn from failures and try again. 1. We must be gritty in our efforts to develop grit in others. KEY TAKEAWAY: Success is not determined by talent alone. Passion, perseverance and continuous effort towards long-term goals are equally important.
