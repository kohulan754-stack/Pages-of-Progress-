GRIT: THE POWER OF PASSION AND PERSEVERANCE Angela Lee Duckworth – TED Talk I. Introduction to the Concept of Grit A. Teaching experience i. Angela Lee Duckworth left management consulting at 27 to become a teacher. ii. Taught seventh-grade mathematics in public schools in New York City. iii. Observed differences in students' academic performance. a. Some high-performing students did not have very high IQ scores. b. Some intelligent students performed poorly. B. Understanding student learning i. Difficult subjects could be learned through sufficient effort and time. ii. Learning depends on motivation and psychological factors. a. Intelligence alone cannot determine academic success. 1. Students need effort, motivation and persistence along with learning ability. II. Research on the Factors of Success A. Duckworth's psychological research i. Studied people in challenging environments to identify the factors behind success. ii. Conducted research in different fields. a. West Point Military Academy – identified cadets likely to complete training. b. National Spelling Bee – studied children's performance in competitions. c. Tough neighbourhood schools – examined teachers' effectiveness and commitment. d. Sales companies – studied employee performance and job retention. B. Common factor identified i. Grit emerged as a significant predictor of success. ii. Success was not primarily determined by: a. IQ or intelligence b. Social intelligence c. Physical health or appearance 1. Grit was the common quality across different challenging environments. III. Meaning and Importance of Grit A. Definition of grit i. Grit is passion and perseverance towards long-term goals. ii. It involves stamina and sustained effort. iii. It means remaining committed to a goal over years. a. Working consistently to achieve one's future ambitions. b. Continuing despite difficulties and setbacks. B. Nature of grit i. Grit requires consistency rather than short-term enthusiasm. ii. It involves dedication and hard work over time. a. Life should be approached like a marathon, not a sprint. 1. Long-term commitment is more important than temporary effort. IV. Grit and Academic Achievement A. Research in Chicago public schools i. Thousands of high school students were assessed through grit questionnaires. ii. Their graduation rates were examined after more than a year. B. Research findings i. Students with higher grit were more likely to graduate. ii. Results remained significant even after considering other factors. a. Family income b. Standardised test scores c. Students' feelings of safety at school 1. Grit also plays a major role in educational success, especially among students at risk of dropping out. V. Developing Grit and the Growth Mindset A. Challenges in building grit i. Science has limited knowledge about how grit can be developed. ii. Parents and teachers seek ways to improve motivation and work ethic. a. Sustaining motivation for long-term goals remains a challenge. B. Talent and grit i. Talent alone does not guarantee success. ii. Talented individuals may fail to complete their commitments. a. Talent is often unrelated or inversely related to grit. 1. Hard work and commitment cannot be replaced by natural ability. C. Growth mindset i. Developed by psychologist Carol Dweck. ii. Belief that learning ability is not fixed and can improve through effort. a. The brain develops and changes in response to challenges. b. Failure is not a permanent condition. 1. Students with a growth mindset are more likely to persevere after failure. VI. Conclusion and Future Approach A. Improving grit i. Growth mindset is a promising approach to developing grit. ii. More research and experiments are required. a. Test ideas, measure results and learn from mistakes. b. Accept failure, correct mistakes and begin again. 1. Developing grit requires persistence in the process of developing persistence itself. KEY TAKEAWAY: Talent may help us begin, but grit helps us continue and achieve long-term goals.
